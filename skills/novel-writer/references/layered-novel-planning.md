# Long-Novel Planning — 全书骨架、分卷精规划与滚动校准

Use this as the canonical planning reference for long, multi-volume, multi-arc, tournament, case-based, journey, career, faction, or changing-cast fiction.

## Core principle

Lock the durable whole-book direction early, plan the current volume and current arc precisely, and delay expendable future detail until it approaches. Do not choose between “plan everything” and “improvise everything.”

Use four layers:

1. whole-book spine;
2. volume plan;
3. current story arc;
4. chapter contracts.

Future plans are intent, not canon. Confirmed prose and approved stable project files remain authoritative.

## 1. Whole-book spine

Lock only the durable direction:

- working title and genre;
- target length range and chapter scale;
- reader promise and Tone Lock from Project Profile;
- protagonist's full-book growth line;
- central conflict and thematic question;
- major volume sequence and final-stage target;
- ending direction or book-ending promise;
- stable world/system rules required from the beginning;
- a compact set of anchor characters, factions, locations, or institutions;
- major prohibitions: reveals that must not occur early, forbidden tropes, and unacceptable ending directions.

Keep this layer compact enough to reread during later volume planning.

## 2. Volume plan

Before drafting a volume, define:

- volume starting state;
- volume goal and endpoint;
- active story arcs;
- chapter-count and word-budget range;
- active cast, temporary cast, and local antagonists;
- important locations, institutions, teams, factions, or systems;
- major set pieces, matches, cases, battles, journeys, or relationship turns;
- long-term foreshadowing touchpoints and local promises;
- what must be resolved in the volume;
- what must remain open;
- exit conditions that establish the next volume's initial state.

Do not fully define later-volume rosters, brackets, minor opponents, transfers, clues, artifacts, or side-character histories unless they affect current decisions.

## 3. Current story arc

For each active arc, record:

- entry state;
- objective;
- opposition or pressure;
- escalation path;
- required cost or consequence;
- turning point;
- climax or decisive choice;
- exit state;
- promises created, advanced, or resolved;
- connection to the volume goal and whole-book spine.

An arc must change story state. Repeated activity without a meaningful state change is not progress.

## 4. Chapter contracts

Translate the current arc into chapter briefs. Each chapter contract owns the local event chain, required characters, knowledge boundaries, allowed foreshadowing movement, prohibited early reveals, emotional turn, ending boundary, and target size.

Do not use the master outline as a substitute for a current chapter contract.

## Rolling expansion

After a volume or major arc:

1. review what actually worked in prose;
2. identify characters, relationships, mechanics, or conflicts that earned future importance;
3. remove or defer unused speculative material;
4. promote only confirmed recurring material into stable canon;
5. run the periodic arc or volume audit in `long-form-continuity.md`;
6. compact active dynamic state into Story Memory;
7. confirm the next volume's starting state and direction;
8. then detail the next volume.

Do not convert early brainstorming into permanent canon merely because it appears in an old planning file.

## Dynamic cast and faction scaling

Use three levels:

### Full-book anchors

Define early only when they shape the entire book:

- protagonist and central relationships;
- primary rival or opposing force;
- core institution, faction, team, family, or organization;
- final-stage destination or antagonist.

### Volume-level participants

Define when their volume approaches:

- role in the current conflict;
- distinctive motive and pressure;
- relevant capabilities and limitations;
- relationship to the protagonist;
- whether they continue, pause, exit, or may recur.

### Functional participants

Create only when needed for a bounded narrative function. Do not give every temporary opponent, official, classmate, soldier, witness, or shopkeeper a full Bible entry.

Promote a functional participant only after prose demonstrates recurring value.

## Event-density planning

For competition, battle, case, mission, or set-piece-heavy fiction, estimate density before locking the volume length.

Classify events:

- full key event: about 3–6 chapters;
- medium event: about 1–3 chapters;
- short showcase: part of one chapter to one chapter;
- summarized event: a scene or short passage.

Also budget chapters for:

- preparation;
- recovery and consequences;
- relationships;
- investigation or learning;
- travel and transition;
- setbacks and changed plans.

When the estimate exceeds the target length, reduce fully dramatized events or expand the project. Do not compress every consequence to preserve an arbitrary chapter count.

## Escalation realism

Recognition, institutional attention, power, resources, and threat scale must follow demonstrated causes.

For example, a local victory may justify local attention, not immediate national or world-level intervention. State the escalation bridge when moving between levels.

## Confirmation

Confirm the whole-book spine before treating it as durable planning. Confirm each volume plan before detailed chapter planning. A direct request to change a future direction changes planning only; it does not silently rewrite established canon or promoted chapters.
