# New-character / New-location Provenance Gate

## Problem
A chapter outline may introduce a new witness, faction site, artifact function, or location that is valid in the global plot but feels abrupt in prose if the protagonists simply go there because the outline says so.

A common failure is introducing a named person or location only because the outline requires it, without an evidence chain that makes the discovery feel earned.

## Rule
Before drafting any chapter, the preflight brief must include a provenance chain for every newly prominent named character/location/faction/artifact:

1. Prior clue or question that creates narrative need.
2. Physical artifact, witness statement, dossier, map, rumor, or institutional link that points toward the new element.
3. Character-specific inference: who can interpret the clue and why their knowledge is bounded.
4. Why the protagonist goes there now, not earlier/later.
5. What remains uncertain so the new element does not become an all-knowing exposition device.

## Good pattern

- Prior clue: an unresolved question points toward an institution or missing witness.
- Artifact: a marked object, residue, signature, map notation, or archived record narrows the search.
- Interpreter: a character can understand only part of the clue because of their established training or history.
- Action reason: the combined evidence explains why the protagonists act now.
- Boundary: the newly found witness or location supplies one bounded piece of information rather than solving the entire plot.

## Bad pattern

- “They went to find the witness” with no prior evidence chain.
- A new witness enters and explains an entire old case because the plot needs exposition.
- A new faction or location appears without being anchored in earlier chapters.

## Preflight checklist snippet
Add a section like:

```markdown
## New Character / Location Provenance
- New element: [name]
- Prior clue(s): [...]
- Artifact / document / witness that points here: [...]
- Interpreter and knowledge boundary: [...]
- Why now: [...]
- What must remain unknown: [...]
```
